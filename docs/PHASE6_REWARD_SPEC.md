| `pump_hydraulic_power_kw` | 79.85 | 0.0 | 104.9 | 0.0 | 323.6 |

**Reading of the scale table** [ENGINEERING DECISION on interpretation of
VALIDATED FACTs]:

- **Objective vs service magnitude gap.** Raw leakage (~85 GPM) is ~10× smaller
  than served/requested demand (~750–900 GPM) and ~4–5× larger than the
  pressure-deficit sum (~18 psi·nodes). A raw weighted sum is dominated by the
  demand terms → **fixed normalisation is mandatory before any weighting**
  (§8.5, §8.6).
- **Leakage moves in a narrow band.** Across the full in-bounds envelope leakage
  spans only 79.92–90.15 GPM (±~6% about baseline). The controllable leakage
  signal is *small in absolute terms* — a strong argument for a
  **baseline-relative / normalised** objective rather than raw GPM.
- **`dsr` and `min_service_pressure` are the discriminating safety/service
  signals.** `dsr` ranges 0.914–1.002; `min_service_pressure` ranges 1.09–5.93
  psi. Both vary meaningfully across configs, so both are usable dense signals.
- **Hard-violation count is degenerate in-bounds.** `n_service_nodes_negative`
  is **identically 0** across all 3667 in-bounds instants → a pure
  hard-violation reward term is **sparse/degenerate**; the *dense* safety signal
  must come from the smooth deficit / min-pressure terms (§8.4).
- **`dsr` can nudge slightly above 1.0** (max 1.00246; `unserved` min −0.07 GPM)
  at the high-pressure corner — a benign PDA artefact where reported consumption
  marginally exceeds the nominal request; a service term should clip
  `unserved := max(0, requested−served)` [ENGINEERING DECISION].
- **Pump power median 0.0, mean ~80 kW, max ~324 kW.** Native STATUS rules keep
  pumps closed a large fraction of the horizon (0 kW), so power is a strongly
  bimodal, native-rule-driven quantity → an energy term is **optional** and, if
  used, normalised by `power_ref` and clearly labelled hydraulic-only.

### Temporal variability (mean within-config std / mean |step-to-step Δ|)

Leakage is temporally smooth (within-config std ≈ 0.23 GPM, |Δ| ≈ 0.026 GPM);
`dsr` |Δ| ≈ 6.4e-4; deficit |Δ| ≈ 0.056 psi·nodes. Demand-driven terms swing
with the diurnal pattern (served |Δ| ≈ 172 GPM). Safety counts barely move
between adjacent steps. This means per-step reward differences are dominated by
demand, not by leakage — reinforcing normalisation and a baseline-relative
objective [VALIDATED FACT / ENGINEERING DECISION].

### Actuator effort / smoothness (analytic) [ENGINEERING DECISION]

From the frozen `[-1,1]^7` box, per-step Δaᵢ∈[−2,2]:
`max L1 = 14.0`, `max L2² = 28.0`, `max L2 ≈ 5.29`. Physical spans per actuator
(psi / rel-speed): 173=45.5, 175=88.5, 176=39.524, 177=70.0, 178=54.0;
PUMP-170=0.28, PUMP-172=0.33. **Realised** movement scale depends on the
not-yet-existing policy trajectory → **[HYPOTHESIS TO TEST]** at training time;
only the analytic ceiling is asserted here.

## 8.4 Cross-correlation and reward-pathology evidence

### Cross-component correlation (pooled Pearson, selected pairs) [VALIDATED FACT]

| Pair | r | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| leakage ↔ dsr | **+0.750** | more leakage co-occurs with higher service (both rise with pressure) |
| leakage ↔ pressure_deficit | **−0.774** | more leakage co-occurs with *less* pressure deficit |
| leakage ↔ n_below20 | **−0.742** | more leakage co-occurs with fewer low-pressure nodes |
| leakage ↔ min_service_pressure | +0.096 | weak |
| dsr ↔ pressure_deficit | **−0.965** | service and deficit are almost mirror images |
| unserved ↔ dsr | −0.736 | consistent (unserved = requested·(1−dsr)) |
| served ↔ requested | +0.999 | both diurnal-demand-driven |
| pump_power ↔ everything | ≤ 0.06 | effectively decoupled (native-rule-driven) |
| n_service_nodes_negative ↔ * | 0.0 | constant column (no variance in-bounds) |

**Central finding:** leakage is **positively** correlated with service/pressure
health (r≈+0.75 with DSR, r≈−0.77 with deficit). Pressing leakage down pushes
pressure down, which erodes service — the two goals **genuinely oppose**. A
reward that does not explicitly protect service will trade service for leakage.

### Pathology checks (measured directions) [VALIDATED FACT]

| Pathology | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Leakage-only collapses service** | `prv_all_lower`: leak 84.73→81.38 GPM (−3.35, −4.0%) but DSR 0.98334→**0.91940** (−6.4 pts); min svc 4.165→4.190 psi; 0 new nodes <20 | leakage-only is unsafe → **service must be protected explicitly** |
| **Pressure-only raises leakage** | `prv_all_upper`: leak 84.73→**89.76** GPM (+5.03, +5.9%); nodes <20 fall 2→1 | objectives oppose; can't just maximise pressure |
| **Scale imbalance** | penalty-term median max/min ratio **7.15**; raw leak(~85) ≫ deficit(~18) ≫ effort(O(1)) | raw weighted sum is dominated → **fixed normalisation required** |
| **Sparse / degenerate signal** | `n_service_nodes_negative` = 0 at **100%** of in-bounds instants | pure hard-violation term is sparse; dense safety = deficit / min-pressure |
| **Actuator-penalty domination** | unnormalised L2² movement up to **28** vs normalised terms ~O(1) | effort MUST be normalised by its analytic max (28) |
| **Reward-hacking / closed-pump ambiguity** | realised pump speed is reset to 1.0 by `STATUS IS OPEN` or 0 when closed (G12) | effort/smoothness MUST use **COMMANDED** normalised action, never realised speed |
| **Boundary-seeking safety** | leakage optimum sits **at the PRV lower bound** (`prv_all_lower`); only this single corner characterised; worst pooled min svc pressure 1.09 psi | a **safety term + STEP-9 hard clamp is required, not optional**; exhaustive joint in-box safety is a STEP-9/STEP-12 job (not claimed here) |

## 8.5 PROPOSED reward formulations (NO weights chosen)

All four are **PROPOSED** [ENGINEERING DECISION] — none is selected, no weights
are tuned, no PPO is trained. They share the fixed normalisation constants of
§8.6 so that every term is ~O(1). `da_t` is the per-step change in the
**commanded** normalised action. Selection + weighting is a **separate approved
decision** (D-P6-005 remains PROPOSED).

**F1 — Normalised weighted sum** (dense, differentiable, standard):
```
r_t = − w_L·(leak_t/leak_ref) − w_U·(unserved_t/req_ref)
      − w_P·(deficit_t/deficit_ref) − w_A·(‖da_t‖²/L2sq_max)
      [− w_E·(power_t/power_ref)]
```
Every term normalised to ~O(1); weights NOT tuned here. Energy term optional
(`w_E` default 0).

**F2 — Constrained penalty, service protected by construction:**
```
r_t = −(leak_t/leak_ref) − Λ·(deficit_t/deficit_ref) − BIG·max(0, n_below20_t)
      with Λ ≫ 1, BIG ≫ Λ
```
Minimise leakage subject to a large explicit service/safety penalty.

**F3 — Baseline-relative improvement** (interpretable, zero-centred at native):
```
r_t = (leak_ref − leak_t)/leak_ref − w_U·max(0, unserved_t − unserved_ref)/req_ref − safety_penalty
```
Reads directly as *fractional leakage reduction vs the native controller*;
robust to the narrow absolute leakage band (§8.3).

**F4 — Safety-first lexicographic** (encodes priority safety > service > leakage
> effort):
```
if any svc node < 20 psi (or < 0):  dominant negative penalty
else:                                minimise leak_t/leak_ref ; effort as tie-break
```
Pairs naturally with the STEP-9 safety clamp (masking / large penalties).

These map onto the CLAUDE.md priority hierarchy (scientific validity → approved
scope → decisions → convenience) and to the domain priority
**safety > service > leakage > effort/energy**. F2/F4 protect service by
construction; F1/F3 protect it via explicit normalised penalties.

## 8.9 Empirical comparison of candidate formulations F1–F4 [MEASUREMENT / D-P6-005 EVIDENCE]

This section reports a **controlled side-by-side comparison** of the four proposed
formulations (§8.5). It produces transparent, reproducible evidence for the
researcher's D-P6-005 decision. It **does not** select, score, rank, or label any
formulation, and it does **not** change §8.5–§8.8 or resolve D-P6-005. All
formulations remain **PROPOSED**.

**Method.** The four rewards were evaluated **per report timestep** over the same
**19 static in-bounds commands** used for the STEP 8 scale analysis (reference,
all-PRV-lower, all-PRV-upper, ten per-PRV edges, two pump edges, four mixed
PRV×pump corners), re-running the validated 96 h protocol (1800 s report / 180 s
rule / ACC 1e-5 / TRIALS 2000 / PDA) and reusing the **validated STEP 8 component
extractor** verbatim. The frozen normalization constants (§8.6) and the derived
`unserved_ref = 14.98484 GPM` are **loaded from the STEP 8 artefact** — no online,
running, or test-set statistics. A reproducibility gate reproduced the frozen
baseline exactly (`leak_ref_abs_diff = 0.0`, `req_ref_abs_diff = 0.0`,
`n_node_ts_below_20psi = 386`, `n_node_ts_negative = 0`,
`per_timestep_vs_validated_accounting_max_abs_diff = 0.0`, all reward values
finite).

**Provisional comparison parameters — `PROVISIONAL FOR COMPARISON ONLY`.** These
are illustrative constants chosen solely to make the four rewards comparable on
one scale; they are **not** proposed weights and carry no recommendation:

| Formulation | Provisional parameters |
|---|---|
| F1 (normalized weighted sum) | `w_L = w_U = w_P = w_A = 1`, `w_E = 0` |
| F2 (constrained penalty) | `Λ = 20`, `BIG = 100` |
| F3 (baseline-relative improvement) | `w_U = 1`, `w_P_safety = 1` |
| F4 (safety-first lexicographic) | `threshold = 20 psi`, `c_soft = 10`, `c_hard = 100`, `eps_effort = 0.01` |

**Empirical findings (descriptive).** To probe incentive direction without
comparing formulations against each other, we report, for each reward, the
**incentive-argmax**: the tested command with the highest mean reward within the
tested set. This is an *incentive direction indicator*, **not** a ranking of
formulations and **not** a claim that the argmax command is a good operating point.

1. **Induced incentive under the provisional parameters.** All four rewards place
   their incentive-argmax on the **same** tested command, `prv_176_upper`
   (VALVE-176 at its upper bound 59.524 psi, others nominal), which sits on an
   action bound and carries `mean n_below20 = 1.0` and `min service ≈ 4.17 psi`.
   Under these provisional weights the incentive is toward *raising* VALVE-176
   (fewer chronically-low nodes, lower deficit) rather than toward the
   leak-minimizing lower corner. Reward spread across tested commands: F1 0.541,
   F3 0.525, F4 0.526; F2 110.5 (dominated by the `BIG·n_below20` term).

2. **Isolated-objective checks (Pathology A/B).** A **leakage-only** incentive
   placed its argmax at `prv_lower_pump_lower`, on the action bound, with worst-node
   service **1.295 psi** (vs reference 4.165 psi). A **service/safety-only** incentive
   placed its argmax at `prv_176_upper` with `leak_norm 1.0155` (> reference 1.0).
   The two objectives pull in opposite directions, consistent with the STEP 8
   correlations (leak↔deficit −0.774).

3. **Hard 20 psi constraint degeneracy (Pathology F/G).** Every pooled timestep has
   at least one service node below 20 psi (`frac = 1.0`), including the native
   baseline (2 chronically low nodes); no timestep has a negative node
   (`frac = 0.0`). Consequently F2 (`BIG·n_below20`) and F4 (`threshold = 20 psi`)
   treat the network's chronic sub-20 nodes as a violation on **every** tested
   command, so the leakage objective receives little/no gradient — an **ill-posed
   hard constraint**. A 0 psi threshold (F4 sensitivity) never triggers in bounds,
   so F4 collapses to pure leak minimization and inherits the
   boundary-seeking-to-lower-PRV incentive while reporting "safe."

4. **Actuator-penalty domination (Pathology C, analytic).** The normalized leak term
   spans only **0.108** across the whole tested set, whereas a unit-weight maximum
   single-step movement costs **1.0**. An un-tuned effort weight could therefore
   dominate the leakage objective. Realised movement is 0 in all static runs, so
   this is **analytic [HYPOTHESIS TO TEST]**; effort/smoothness must be computed on
   the **commanded** normalized action (realised pump speed is reset by STATUS-IS
   rules per G12 and is gameable).

5. **Weight/penalty sensitivity (Pathology H).** Varying the provisional parameters
   moves the incentive-argmax: e.g. F1 with `w_P = 0` shifts the argmax to
   `prv_175_lower` (leak-leaning), while `w_P ≥ 0.5` shifts it to `prv_176_upper`;
   F3 with both safety weights 0 shifts to `prv_lower_pump_lower`. This sensitivity
   is expected and is precisely why weight selection is deferred to the researcher.

## 8.11 Safety implication (belongs to STEP 9, not STEP 8) [ENGINEERING NOTE]

The isolated-objective and boundary-seeking checks show a **leakage-driving reward
sends its incentive to the PRV lower corner with worst-node service ≈ 1.3 psi**.
This confirms that a **state-dependent safety mechanism is required** — but that is
**STEP 9 (unapproved)** and is **not** added here. STEP 8 keeps the fixed action
**bounds** (STEP 6, D-P6-001/002/003) distinct from a state-dependent safety
**clamp**; no clamp was inserted into any formulation or into the environment. The
chronic sub-20 baseline (2 nodes) is a **formulation-design consideration**, not a
constraint the environment can currently satisfy.

## 8.12 A2 resolution (2026-10-01) — movement-term action under safety intervention [D-P6-006 companion; D-P6-005 UNCHANGED]

**Open question (WU-18 §14).** Under a STEP-9 safety intervention the *proposed*
(pre-safety) action and the *executed* (post-safety) action differ. The frozen reward
movement term `w_A·(‖da_t‖²/movement_L2sq_max)` must be computed from one of them. This
section fixes which, as a **companion item to D-P6-006**. It **does not** modify the
frozen D-P6-005 formula, weights (`w_A=0.1`), or `movement_L2sq_max=28.0`.

**Resolution — the movement term uses the EXECUTED (post-safety) action, which is
exactly what D-P6-005 already means by "COMMANDED":**

1. **D-P6-005 wording.** D-P6-005 defines `da_t = commanded normalised action_t −
   previous commanded normalised action`. "Commanded" = the normalised action actually
   **issued to the actuators** that drives the EPANET transition = the **executed
   (post-safety)** action. The only quantity D-P6-005 / §8.1 excluded was the **realised
   pump speed** (reset to ~1.0/0 by native STATUS-IS rules per G12, hence gameable) —
   **not** the proposed pre-safety action.
2. **Reward-internal consistency.** Every other reward term (`leak`, `unserved⁺`,
   `deficit`, `power`) is computed from the **executed** hydraulic state (the state the
   executed action produced). The movement term must therefore also use the executed
   action so the whole per-step reward measures the consequences of **what was actually
   done**, not of a proposal that was overridden.
3. **Physical meaning.** The actuators physically move by `a_exec,t − a_exec,t−1`.
   Penalising `a_prop,t` would charge the policy for movement that never physically
   occurred.
4. **Observation consistency (D-P6-004).** The frozen observation's `prev_cmd_*` blocks
   (`prev_cmd_prv_setpoint[123:128]`, `prev_cmd_pump_speed[133:135]`) store the previous
   **executed** command. `da_t = a_exec,t − a_exec,t−1` is self-consistent with those
   fields; using `a_prop` would reference a quantity the observation does not carry.

**This is a disambiguation + documentation, NOT a change.** Formula, weights, and the
five frozen normalisation constants are unchanged; D-P6-005 stays frozen.

**Train/execute gap (monitor item, not a reward change).** On an intervened step with
H1 reject-to-hold, `a_exec,t = a_prev`, so `da_t = a_prev − a_exec,t−1`; if the agent was
already holding, this is `0` and the step incurs **no** movement cost. This is **benign**
(no actuator moved ⇒ no movement cost) and **not** an exploitable incentive: the agent
cannot *choose* to be rejected (rejection fires only on an unsafe proposal), and the
dominant `leak`/`unserved`/`deficit` terms still reflect the held hydraulic state. The
proposed-vs-executed gap is a **train/execute observability** concern handled at the PPO
layer (log BOTH `a_prop` and `a_exec`), **not** by redefining the frozen reward.
